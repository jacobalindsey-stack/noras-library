# Georgia Buns Game Bible

Updated Nov 19, 2026 · @Jacob Lindsey

## Origin and tone

The Georgia Buns are 17 bunny cousins (all boys) in a forested version of Atlanta, from bedtime stories told to your daughter starting about six years ago (she's 8 now). They draw on people and experiences from growing up in rural Georgia in the 1980s: silly stories about simple people that get complicated by the situation.

Tone is irreverent, warm and absurd. In the songs, the rule held firm: everything should feel like real adult entertainment, never a kids' product.

**Canon vs. song invention:** Everything marked *(canon)* came from you directly. Everything marked *(song)* was invented for the lyrics and is optional for the game. *(game)* marks things invented for the game.

## The world's subtext *(canon)*

The model is how Schitt's Creek treats gay rights: the show inhabits a world with zero stigma and total normalization, and never discusses it. The Georgia Buns do the same with race. Theirs is an idealized South where racial tension simply doesn't exist, and the things Black and white Southerners share (food, music, vernacular) are just how life is.

- **Elevate, never punch down.** The point is to celebrate regional culture at a time of growing homogeneity. Nothing stereotypes to demean or look down.
- **Related to the world of Uncle Remus, but coming from a different place.** Deeply regional animal stories, told with respect.
- **Never discussed, always present.** It lives in the cookout, the grill, the collards, the playlist that goes from rap to country, and the rhythm of how the buns talk. No character ever remarks on it.
- **Craft note:** vernacular comes through in word choice, rhythm and timing, not in phonetically spelled-out dialect.

## World and setting

The home map is the forest, in a forestified ATL. *(canon)*

**The house** *(canon)*: a hybrid of a home and an underground burrow. It has a living room and a kitchen island; windows look out onto a lawn, and beyond the lawn is the forest, where most of the action takes place. Roof Bun lives on the roof. A garden out back.

**The forest and beyond** *(canon)*: the forest, a creek running through it, and a lake beyond that. Sunny's tree is right outside the house.

**ATL** *(canon)*: a city on a blurred boundary between the human and animal worlds. It has everything the buns like, including the clubs, which they drive through in the Escalade.

## Food and music *(canon)*

- **Food:** ribs, grilling and barbecue; above all collards, and of course carrots (and carrot juice). Carrots double as money.
- **Music:** they love rap and they love country.

## The Buns

Seventeen cousins, all boys. None have names; some have specific roles and go by them. *(canon)*

- Love food: carrots, collards, ribs, barbecue
- Love rap and country; love Louis Vuitton and ice-drip; drive an Escalade
- Throw parties with carrot juice
- Driven by desires that aren't always logical: a bun can wake up with a burning need to throw rocks into the creek, and won't be stopped until it's done
- Start with a clear goal, then lose the plot; argue over misunderstandings or clashing goals

**Roof Bun** *(canon)* lives on the roof. In the game he's gray and wears shades.

**Nibbin' Bun** *(canon)* has uncontrollable urges to munch on things, furniture included. In the game he's tan with buck teeth.

**The other fifteen** have no fixed roles. In the game each is defined by his desire of the day, in different fur colors (brown, white, ginger, dark, smoke, honey). They all wear gold chains; the hero's pendant is the drip you pick at The Ice Box (carrot, gold rope or iced out). *(game)*

**The lone cousin** *(episode)* is the one stuck at baggage claim in Paris; in the game he's ginger with a gold rope.

## Adventure formula *(canon)*

1. **A bun wants something.** Often illogical.
2. **It goes awry.** Something gets forgotten, or a new goal comes into focus.
3. **Miscommunication or mishap** sends some or all of the cousins the wrong way.
4. **Physical comedy** and sometimes an argument.
5. **It lands somewhere unexpected**, often better or funnier than planned.

## Game direction (decided Nov 18, 2026)

- **Format:** a side-scrolling speed adventure, Sonic as the touchstone (Mania-grade pixel art, parallax, slopes and loops, music), plus one into-the-screen driving day.
- **Structure:** a burrow hub (pebble phone, six postcards, sticker shelf) and six days, plus a secret seventh behind the burrow window. One hero per day, quest-based: three items to find, places to reach, an animal to deal with in a specific way. Every day ends at sundown with a cookout.
- **Speed is sacred.** Glancing hits deflect, small rocks get vaulted, big ones bounce you into a tumble that lands on its feet. No death.
- **Carrots are the rings** and the money.
- **Secrets:** three golden carrots per day on secret paths (21 with Boulder), and cameo stickers hidden in the world: Nora's other games (Wick, Luna, Biscuit, the navy Volvo, a slashable pumpkin, the Cinnamon Roll Cat, a clock stuck at 11:59, a sphinx fragment, two moons), Georgia (the peach stand, the old red tractor, Spaghetti Junction, Stone Mountain, the big New Year's peach, a pink lawn flamingo, the buns' mailbox), a French-touch gold record, and Boulder (Sunny's Boulder squirrel, a pair of skis).
- **Controls:** floating stick plus one JUMP button; EASY switch for hold-where-you-want-to-go. iPad and PC; iPhone held sideways.

## The saga (built Nov 19, 2026, beta) *(game)*

The six days were bones. The game now wraps them in a family saga: the side-scrolling levels became places you go back to, and the runs are the action scenes inside a longer story about seventeen cousins getting what they want.

**The loop.** Every morning a few cousins wake up wanting something (canon: "a bun can wake up with a burning need to throw rocks into the creek"). You get three suns a day; each run spends one. Idle cousins go on errands and come back at sundown with whatever they *think* you asked for. Every day ends with a cookout, wherever you ended up. Then it's morning again.

**Earning a name.** Only the Roof Bun and the Nibbin' Bun have names at the start. Each of the other fifteen has one big Want; when he gets it, he earns his role and the player picks it from three choices (for example THE CREEK BUN, THE SKIPPER or THE ROCK BUN). The family screen counts "N of 17 known." This is the game's answer to the open question about the other fifteen: the player (Nora) names them.

**Mission types.** The same levels host different runs: *fetch* (the original three items), *haul* (carry something to the end; bumps knock it loose and you go back for it), *roundup* (wake cousins and they follow you in a line; bumps shake a couple loose), *sprint* (beat the sundown; the sky darkens with the clock), *toll* (bring enough carrots), *hunt* (one lost thing; the pebble pings when you're close), *drive* (Peachtree). Plus a *reverse* run: haul shingles from the creek back up to the house. No run can be lost; how well it went is one to three stars.

**Quest drift** *(canon formula)*. Some Wants hit a fork mid-way. Carrot stew (the unbuilt canon episode) gets the vegetables, then: "Forgot the onions. Also the roof is leaking." Stick to the stew, or fix the roof. Both finish the Want with different endings, rewards and names. The family screen tallies STUCK TO IT and LOST THE PLOT.

**Errands and quirks.** Each of the fifteen has a quirk, discovered the first time he runs an errand: show-off, gets lost, country, sleepy, fancy, hungry, dreamer, literal, haggler, musical, social, lucky, speedy, handy, nervous. Ask for ice: the literal one brings googly eyes ("real eyes"), the fancy one brings a pinky ring and spends your carrots, the lost one ends up in a diner in Macon. The Nibbin' Bun brings everything back with bite marks. The Roof Bun never comes down.

**The cookout.** Errand reports, then the spread (up to six dishes from the pantry), then the vibes: food, family, friends (guests earned through Wants), stuff (the stash), and today's stars. Tiers run from QUIET NIGHT to BIGGEST IN GEORGIA, and the tip jar pays carrots.

**The map.** The Forest (home: creek and garden runs), Peachtree in ATL (the drive, The Ice Box, Peachtree Customs, the Corner Market), Elli's farm in Washington GA (the peach stand), Sullivan's Island (Pontoon's shack), Paris (Maison Carotte), Boulder (Boulder Gear). Places open through pebble phone calls: Dexter, Pontoon, the airline ("who booked these?"), the Boulder bunnies.

**The stash: coveted things.** Drip (gold rope, carrot pendant, iced out, tennis chain, pinky ring, ear stud, sand dollar chain), finery (orange box, monogram duffel and trunk, silk scarf, beret, beanie, sunhat), the ride (twenty-fours, hydraulics, neon underglow, subwoofers, and the bumper the Nibbin' Bun got to), home (the big grill, smoker, string lights, the Escargots' old speaker, disco ball, porch swing, kiddie pool, the hammock, tiny skis), the album on vinyl, and trophies from Wants. Some have perks: the gold rope pulls carrots in, the iced-out chain shows golden carrots on the trip meter, the duffel forgives one haul drop, Real Eyes stop Sunny's acorns ("she can't tell where you're looking"), the hammock makes every day four suns. Brands stay off screen: "monogram," never the real name.

**The finale.** When all seventeen are known: THE BIGGEST COOKOUT IN GEORGIA, a garden roundup with the whole family running behind you in one line, then everyone at the cookout and credits that list every cousin by the name the player gave him. The game keeps going after.
